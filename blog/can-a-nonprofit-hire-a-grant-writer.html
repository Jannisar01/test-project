Can a Nonprofit Hire Someone to Write Grants?

By Holly Selwitschka · Updated June 2026 · 5 min read

Yes — a nonprofit can absolutely hire someone to write grants. You can bring on a freelance grant writer or grant-writing consultant to research funding opportunities, write the proposals, and handle reporting after you win. You don't need a big development department or a full-time hire to do it. In fact, most of the nonprofits that work with a grant writer are small and mid-sized organizations without one.

Key takeaways

  • Nonprofits of any size can hire a grant writer — freelance, contract, or part-time.
  • A grant writer researches funders, writes proposals, and supports reporting.
  • You stay in control of your mission and budget; the writer handles the heavy lifting.
  • Hiring one makes sense when time, capacity, or expertise is the bottleneck.

Why so many nonprofits don't realize this is an option

Here's the quiet truth: many capable, mission-driven nonprofits never pursue grant funding simply because no one on staff has the time or training to write a competitive proposal. Leaders often assume grant writing is something you either do in-house or don't do at all. It isn't. Grant writing is a professional service you can hire for the same way you hire a bookkeeper, a web designer, or a fractional CFO.

What does a grant writer actually do?

A grant writer's job covers the full funding lifecycle. In practice, that usually breaks into three parts:

  • Grant research: identifying funders — community foundations, family foundations, government agencies, and corporate giving programs — whose priorities match your mission and budget.
  • Proposal writing: drafting clear, funder-focused applications that answer exactly what each funder asks, including the narrative, budget, and supporting documents.
  • Reporting & compliance: after an award, preparing progress reports and impact statements so you stay in good standing and remain eligible for future funding.

Crucially, a grant writer doesn't replace your voice — they translate the work you already do into the language funders fund.

How does hiring a grant writer work?

Most engagements start with a short, no-cost conversation — sometimes called a discovery call or listening session — where the writer learns about your programs, your funding history, and your goals. From there, you might work together on a single proposal, a season of applications, or an ongoing retainer. You can scale the relationship up or down as your capacity and pipeline change.

Is it worth hiring a grant writer?

It's usually worth it in three situations: when your team is leaving grant money on the table because no one has time to apply; when you want to go after larger or more complex grants than you can write internally; or when past proposals haven't landed and you want stronger, more competitive applications. A skilled grant writer protects your staff's time and raises the quality of every submission — which, over a year, can more than pay for itself in funding secured and hours saved.

What to look for in a grant writer

  • Understanding of your world. Someone who knows how nonprofits actually operate will write more convincingly than a generalist.
  • Honesty about outcomes. No ethical grant writer guarantees a win — funding is competitive. Be wary of anyone who promises results.
  • Transparent pricing. Ethical grant writers charge by the hour, project, or retainer — never a percentage of the grant. (More on that in our cost guide.)
  • A listening-first approach. The best proposals start with your mission, not a template.
