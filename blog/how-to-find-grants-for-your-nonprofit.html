How to Find Grant Opportunities for Your Nonprofit

By Holly Selwitschka· Updated June 2026· 7 min read

To find grants for your nonprofit, define exactly what you need funding for, then search community foundations, government portals like Grants.gov, grant databases, and funder 990s — and vet each funder for fit before you apply. The goal isn't a giant list of every grant in existence. It's a short, realistic list of funders who actually fund organizations like yours.

Key takeaways

  • Start from your need, not from a database — clarity makes searching faster.
  • Your local community foundation is often the best first stop.
  • Fit beats volume: 10 well-matched funders outperform 100 random ones.
  • A simple grant calendar turns scattered effort into a repeatable pipeline.

Step 1: Define your funding need

Before you search, get specific. Are you funding a single program, general operating costs, new equipment, or organizational capacity? Funders give very differently depending on the type of support, so naming your need up front saves hours and sharpens every search that follows.

Step 2: Search the right sources

Cast a focused net across these proven sources:

  • Your community foundation. Local foundations fund local nonprofits — and they're often the most accessible starting point for small and mid-sized organizations.
  • Government portals. Grants.gov lists federal opportunities; your state arts board, humanities council, and agencies list state grants.
  • Grant databases. Tools like Candid's Foundation Directory and Instrumentl aggregate funders; many public libraries provide free access.
  • Foundation 990s. A funder's tax filing (free on Candid or ProPublica) reveals exactly who they've funded and how much — a goldmine for spotting fit.
  • Peers and word of mouth. Look at who funds organizations similar to yours, and ask your network.

Step 3: Vet each funder for fit

This is where most wasted effort gets cut. Before adding a funder to your list, confirm four things:

  1. Geography — do they fund your region?
  2. Mission alignment — do your programs match their priorities?
  3. Grant size — is their typical award in a range that's worth your time?
  4. Eligibility — do you meet their requirements (501(c)(3) status, budget size, restrictions)?

Applying only where you genuinely fit is the single biggest lever on your success rate.

Step 4: Build a grant calendar

Turn your vetted list into a simple spreadsheet: funder, deadline, award range, requirements, contact, and status. A grant calendar transforms last-minute scrambles into a steady, repeatable pipeline — and makes it obvious where to focus next.

A note on time

Done well, grant research takes real hours — reading guidelines, comparing 990s, confirming eligibility. That's exactly the work a grant writer takes off your plate, handing you a curated, ready-to-apply list instead of a browser full of tabs.
