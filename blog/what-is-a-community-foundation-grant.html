What Is a Community Foundation Grant — and How Do You Apply?

By Holly Selwitschka· Updated June 2026· 6 min read

A community foundation grant is funding from a community foundation — a local public charity that pools donations from many donors to support nonprofits in a specific region. For small and mid-sized nonprofits, these grants are often the single most accessible place to start, because community foundations care first and foremost about local impact.

Key takeaways

  • Community foundations fund nonprofits within a defined geographic area.
  • They support a wide range of causes — from the arts to human services.
  • Applications are often simpler than large federal grants.
  • Many welcome a conversation before you apply — use it.

How community foundations work

A community foundation gathers charitable gifts — sometimes from hundreds of local donors and families — into a collection of funds, then awards grants from those funds to nonprofits serving the area. Because their mission is the well-being of a specific place, they fund broadly: education, the arts, women's and family services, community development, health, and more. If your work strengthens the local community, there's likely a fit.

Why they're ideal for smaller nonprofits

Three reasons community foundations punch above their weight for growing organizations:

  • Accessibility. Applications tend to be shorter and less technical than federal grants.
  • Relationships. Program officers are local, approachable, and genuinely want to help you succeed.
  • Range. They fund operating support and smaller projects that big funders often won't.

How to apply, step by step

  1. Find your local community foundation. Most regions have at least one; a quick search for "[your county or city] community foundation" usually finds it.
  2. Read the grant guidelines. Note focus areas, eligibility, award sizes, and deadlines.
  3. Confirm you're eligible. Check geography, mission fit, and 501(c)(3) status.
  4. Reach out. Many foundations invite a call or letter of inquiry first — a chance to confirm fit before investing in a full proposal.
  5. Submit a focused proposal. Tell a clear story: the need, your solution, who benefits, and how you'll measure impact.

What a strong application looks like

Community foundations fund stories grounded in evidence. Lead with the local need, show how your program meets it, and back it with concrete outcomes. Keep the language plain and human — these are neighbors funding neighbors. A grant writer can help you shape that narrative so it lands the first time.
