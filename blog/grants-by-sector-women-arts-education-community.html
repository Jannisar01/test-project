By Sector

Grants for Women's Services, Arts, Education & Community Nonprofits

By Holly Selwitschka· Updated June 2026· 7 min read

The best grants for your nonprofit depend on your mission — but every sector has a natural set of funders to start with. Below is a practical map for four mission areas: women's services, the arts, education, and community building. Use it to point your search in the right direction, then vet each funder for local fit.

Key takeaways

  • Women's services: women's foundations, giving circles, family foundations, government programs.
  • Arts: state arts councils, the NEA, humanities councils, local arts agencies.
  • Education: education foundations, corporate literacy/STEM programs, government grants.
  • Community building: community foundations, United Way, civic and corporate giving.

Women's services nonprofits

If you serve women and families, start with women's foundations and giving circles — funders built specifically to advance women's well-being and equity. Add family foundations with a gender-equity focus, government programs tied to domestic violence prevention, housing, or workforce development, and your local community foundation. Local women's funds tend to be especially welcoming to small organizations.

Arts & culture nonprofits

Arts organizations have a rich funding landscape. Begin with your state arts council and the National Endowment for the Arts, then explore humanities councils, local arts agencies, and community foundations with dedicated arts-and-culture funds. Many corporate giving programs also love supporting visible, local arts work that brings communities together.

Education nonprofits

For education and youth programs, look to education-focused foundations, corporate programs in literacy, STEM, and college access, and government education grants at the state and federal levels. Community foundations frequently fund after-school, tutoring, and early-childhood programs that strengthen local schools and families.

Community-building nonprofits

If your work knits a community together, your strongest allies are community foundations and United Way, alongside civic organizations, corporate community-investment programs, and capacity-building funders. These funders prize measurable local impact and collaboration — so highlight partnerships and outcomes.

The thread that runs through all four

Whatever your sector, two things consistently win funding: a clear, evidence-backed story of local need, and proof you can deliver results. The funders change; the craft of a compelling, funder-focused proposal does not. That's the part a grant writer is built to handle.
